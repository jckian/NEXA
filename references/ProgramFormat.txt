Core generation rules:

- Total number of "fire stair" types must match required exit stair count (min 3, preferred 4).
- Core 1:
  {fire stair and freight elevator}/~150–250/each floor/circulation/{~10,15}
  - Located near loading/service side
  - Connected to back-of-house circulation
- Core 2+:
  Each defined as:
  {fire stair and elevator 1}/~120–180/each floor/circulation/{~10,12}
  {fire stair and elevator 2}/~120–180/each floor/circulation/{~10,12}
  {fire stair and elevator 3}/~120–180/each floor/circulation/{~10,12}
- Distribution:
  - Must satisfy ≥ 1/2 floor diagonal separation
  - Evenly distributed across floor plate

- SMALL-FLOORPLATE VARIANT (added 260729):
  The areas above were calibrated on TPAC, whose floor plates run several thousand m2. On a
  plate of roughly 1,000 m2 or less, three cores at 150 m2 each eat close to half the floor,
  which is not a core, it is the building. So:

    plate > 2,000 m2   use the areas above (150-250 service, 120-180 passenger)
    plate 1,000-2,000  service 90-150, passenger 70-120
    plate < 1,000 m2   service 45-70,  passenger 35-60

  The exit-stair COUNT does not scale down: min 3 stays min 3 regardless of plate size,
  because it comes from egress, not from area. Only the area per core scales.

  This is a sizing convention for study models, not a code calculation. A real core is sized
  from stair width, lift count and shaft services; these numbers exist so a generated program
  reads at the right proportion on a small plate. The NEXA intel-layer scenarios use 40 m2
  cores on ~1,000 m2 plates, which sits just under the small-plate band and is acceptable at
  study scale.
